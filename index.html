Human correction

One sentence becomes a short bias on a frozen policy.

A person watches one failed rollout and writes one sentence. It becomes a short bias on the frozen policy’s actions and is reused on every later rollout, with no retraining and no new data.

  1. 1

    Watch

    One failed rollout, one sentence (one or two clauses for most tasks).

  2. 2

    Ground

    GPT-5 mini turns each clause into a dimension, sign, magnitude, and time window.

  3. 3

    Shape

    The magnitude word sets a weight, ramped up and down within the window.

  4. 4

    Replay

    The bias is added to the frozen policy on every later rollout.

Where the magnitude ranges come from

Each word maps to a fixed fraction of the controller’s largest step, set empirically from a few simulation trials. The ranges depend on the robot and could be learned.

What the signal assumes

Does the signal suffice?

Where the sentence reaches, it usually suffices.

Simulation: LIBERO, π0.5

Corrections are written for execution-misalignment tasks and re-run on the base policy’s failed episodes. The VLM baseline (GPT-5 mini) gets the same failure video, with no person in the loop.

Real robot: xArm7, π0.5-DROID

π0.5-DROID reached 95% on pick-and-place in its fine-tuning workspace. We then moved the robot base and ran 3 runs of 10 trials per condition, with one sentence per run, written in 40–80 s.

Where the taxonomy says to stop. Folding a shirt and stacking boxes stay at 0/30 with or without correction: they need a plan rather than a magnitude.

Discussion

Where this goes next.

Limitations

  • One annotator labelled the taxonomy.
  • One correction per task assumes one failure pattern and timing per task.
  • Corrections were translational, language-only, and written by the authors; a user study is future work.
  • Magnitude ranges were set empirically; learning them is future work.
  • In simulation, the bias was applied only to failed episodes.
